# Explanation

* [Why four skill roles rather than one skill](why-four-roles.md) - Why the work is split into four skills, sidecar, librarian, curator and docent, named for the library and museum professions and run first in order and then as a loop, beside a fifth role that is not a skill (the registrar) and a fifth skill that is not a role (ktl-prose).
* [Why a registrar role, and why it is not a skill](why-a-registrar-role.md) - What the registrar does, keeping every bundle record in order and every confirmation tied to a person, and why no person has to do it: the `lokf` toolkit, CI's `knowledge-registrar.yaml`, the `knowledge-apply.sh` pen at the librarian's desk, and two optional Obsidian plugins for a desk with no CI.
* [Why the skills live in their own repository](why-a-distribution-repository.md) - Why the five skills are published from one installable repository: three install routes plus Microsoft 365 Copilot for the docent, one tag for all of them, one changelog, a bundle of its own that the docent answers from, and the repository's rename.
* [Hosts and doorways - where the bundle's real folder lives](hosts-and-doorways.md) - Why the bundle is `.lokf/knowledge`, one real folder on every host, with `knowledge_bundle` beside it as a link for people and folder pickers; what the visible layout tried and why it was retired; and what a shared folder that is not a vault may still do by hand.
* [Three lines of defence - where each role sits, and what an auditor can check](three-lines-of-defence.md) - The librarian, curator, registrar, sidecar and docent placed in the IIA's Three Lines Model, for whoever adopts KTL: who owns a claim, what a machine checks, what a person can examine afterwards, the four limits on that evidence, what the critics page answers, and what remains to do and who does it.
* [When the built-in vocabulary stops fitting - domain schemas](domain-schemas.md) - How a bundle in a specialised or regulated domain goes beyond LOKF's core classes: the signs a domain schema is due, a LinkML schema importing LOKF's, named by one `schema` variable in the sidecar's justfile and checked with `lokf validate --schema`, reusing a domain's own vocabulary such as the AI Risk Ontology, the Microsoft 365 identity shape held in reserve, and who raises, decides and applies it.
* [What Knowledge Trust Ladder is for, and what it is not](intended-uses.md) - A draft placeholder for a reader's question no source settles: which uses Knowledge Trust Ladder is meant for, such as DevSecOps or technology governance, and which it is not. The README and `docs/context-layer.md` state the purpose and name no use case or non-goal, and `docs/skills-repositories.md` answers it only for a repository whose content is Agent Skills.
* [What KTL took from WikiSkill](wikiskill.md) - What Knowledge Trust Ladder (KTL) took from the WikiSkill paper (Tang et al., 2026), as `docs/wikiskill.md` says: the same loop of maintainer, pen, consumer and gate; the seven changes the reading made to the skills, such as a consumer that writes nothing, a bounded run and a retrieval score; and what the paper leaves open.
* [What KTL took from SkillWiki](skillwiki.md) - What Knowledge Trust Ladder (KTL) took from the SkillWiki paper (Huang et al., 2026) and its code, as `docs/skillwiki.md` says: the four changes the reading made to the skills, the excerpt check that waits for LOKF 0.9.0, and where the two designs part, such as who stands behind a decision.
* [Knowledge Trust Ladder in a skills repository](skills-repositories.md) - Whether to install Knowledge Trust Ladder (KTL) in a repository whose content is Agent Skills, as `docs/skills-repositories.md` answers: what it adds (the knowledge around the skills), what it leaves to evaluations (whether a skill works), what it costs when skills change weekly, a run on anthropics/skills, how the librarian maps skills, and what remains to do.
* [The curated context layer: one bundle, and a federation of bundles](context-layer.md) - What the industry means by a context layer, why one KTL bundle is already one repository's context layer and where it stops, and a design for federating bundles into one layer: domains as thin LOKF bundles that select releases, and a workspace rebuilt on each release with one named graph per bundle. It also says what exists today, the decisions an adopter makes, and which SPARQL stores, catalogues, agent surfaces and artifact repositories such a layer can use.

# Explanation

* [Why four skill roles rather than one skill](why-four-roles.md) - Why the work is split into four skills, sidecar, librarian, curator and docent, named for the library and museum professions and run first in order and then as a loop, beside a fifth role that is not a skill (the registrar) and two optional skills beside the roles (ktl-prose, and ktl-website, which publishes the bundle).
* [Why a registrar role, and why it is not a skill](why-a-registrar-role.md) - What the registrar, the fifth role and not a skill, does: it keeps every bundle record in order and ties every confirmation to a person, through the `lokf` toolkit, CI's `knowledge-registrar.yaml`, the pen `knowledge-apply.sh` and `knowledge-report.sh`, while the KTL Registrar plugin does that work in Obsidian, where there is no CI.
* [Why the skills live in their own repository](why-a-distribution-repository.md) - Why the six skills are published from one installable repository: three install routes plus Microsoft 365 Copilot for the docent, one tag for all of them, one changelog, a bundle of its own that the docent answers from, and the repository's rename.
* [Why the bundle is in a hidden folder](hosts-and-doorways.md) - Why the bundle lives in the hidden folder `.lokf/knowledge`, the same on every host, and how `knowledge_bundle` beside it, the doorway, lets people, folder pickers and Obsidian find it; the four common arrangements, from a code repository to several repositories in one Obsidian vault; and why the bundle is never a visible folder inside a vault.
* [Three lines of defence - where each role sits, and what an auditor can check](three-lines-of-defence.md) - The librarian, curator, registrar, sidecar and docent placed in the IIA's Three Lines Model, for whoever adopts KTL: who owns a claim, what a machine checks, what a person can examine afterwards, the four limits on that evidence, the model's critics with KTL's answer to each, and what remains to do and who does it.
* [When the built-in vocabulary stops fitting: domain vocabularies](domain-schemas.md) - How a bundle in a specialised or regulated domain goes beyond LOKF's core classes: the signs a domain vocabulary is due, a LinkML file importing LOKF's, kept in `.lokf/schema/`, named by one `schema` variable in the sidecar's justfile and checked with `lokf validate --schema`, a copy adapted there from a LinkML project's domain schema, the `domain.yaml` the sidecar ships for a bundle that federates other bundles, reusing a domain's own schema such as the AI Risk Ontology through `lokf adapt`, the Microsoft 365 identity shape held in reserve, and who raises, decides and applies it.
* [What Knowledge Trust Ladder is for, and what it is not](intended-uses.md) - Which uses Knowledge Trust Ladder (KTL) fits and which it does not, as `docs/uses.md` says: the nine uses the skills were built for, among them knowledge retold for a younger reader, DevSecOps and technology governance, what KTL is not (a scanner, a judge of truth, a vector store, a live system), and three questions for judging any other use.
* [What KTL took from WikiSkill](wikiskill.md) - What Knowledge Trust Ladder (KTL) took from the WikiSkill paper (Tang et al., 2026), and what the paper leaves open. Both run one loop of maintainer, pen, consumer and gate, and the reading made seven changes to the skills, such as a consumer that writes nothing, a bounded run and a retrieval score.
* [What KTL took from SkillWiki](skillwiki.md) - What Knowledge Trust Ladder (KTL) took from the SkillWiki paper (Huang et al., 2026) and its code: the four changes the reading made to the skills, the excerpt check that arrived with LOKF 0.9.0, and where the two designs part, such as who stands behind a decision.
* [A skills repository](skills-repositories.md) - Whether to install Knowledge Trust Ladder (KTL) in a repository whose content is Agent Skills, as `docs/skills-repositories.md` answers: what it adds (the knowledge around the skills), what it leaves to evaluations (whether a skill works), what it costs when skills change weekly, this repository's six skills and a run on anthropics/skills, how the librarian maps skills, and what remains to do.
* [The curated context layer: one bundle, and a federation of bundles](context-layer.md) - What a context layer is, and why one KTL bundle is already one repository's context layer and where it stops. How bundles federate into one: domains as thin LOKF bundles that select releases, tiers of domains and the diamond rule, a workspace with one named graph per bundle served over SPARQL and MCP, and the products it can use.
* [When the built-in vocabulary stops fitting: domain vocabularies](domain-vocabularies.md) - How a bundle in a specialised or regulated domain goes beyond LOKF's core classes: the signs a domain vocabulary is due, a LinkML file importing LOKF's, kept in `.lokf/schema/`, named by one `schema` variable in the sidecar's justfile and checked with `lokf validate --schema`, a copy adapted there from a LinkML project's domain schema, the `domain.yaml` the sidecar ships for a bundle that federates other bundles, reusing a domain's own schema such as the AI Risk Ontology through `lokf adapt`, the Microsoft 365 identity shape held in reserve, and who raises, decides and applies it.
* [Young readers](young-readers.md) - How KTL retells knowledge that adults wrote for readers aged 8 to 18, for a teacher or a youth leader: what a retelling is, the two age bands and their limits, what each role does, how to ask for one, what KTL never does with a young reader, and what exists today.
* [Portability: what works on each platform](portability.md) - What the skills keep, lose and use instead on each platform, as the sidecar's `references/portability.md` lists them: Linux, macOS, Windows, other forges, git with no forge, no git, an Obsidian vault, a package mirror or proxy, and Microsoft 365 Copilot; and how the preflight says which case a machine is.

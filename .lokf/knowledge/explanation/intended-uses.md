# Overview

The README says what the skills are for. Knowledge Trust Ladder keeps a repository's scattered knowledge as a collection: catalogued, authenticated and explained, with the trust in every claim left visible. The knowledge already exists in code, documents, diagrams, policies and operational records. What is missing is a context layer, a governed layer between those sources and whoever consumes them, a person or an agent, that stays put. Without it every task starts the same way, and the work dies with the task.

# What it is

A knowledge bundle is the catalogue, and the trust ladder is the answer to one question: who is responsible for the quality of this context? Every concept says where it came from and how far it has been checked. The README states the value as "prose, structure, meaning, tools": prose a person reads, structure a schema checks, meaning a graph can query, and tools that come with the standard rather than with this project.

# What it is not

The README's credits say this is an independent project, not affiliated with, endorsed by, or an official distribution of LOKF or of the Open Knowledge Format. Nothing in it invents a field, a format or a validator. The curator is always a person; the skill and the plugin that carry the name are that person's assistants, and neither reaches a verdict of its own.

# Where it fits in governance

`docs/three-lines.md` reads the family through the Three Lines Model used in regulated industries, and `docs/three-lines-critics.md` sets the model's critics against what a bundle answers. Those pages say how a bundle fits a governance reading. They do not say which fields of use the skills were built for, which is the open question below.

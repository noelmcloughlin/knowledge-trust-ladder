# Knowledge Trust Ladder for younger readers

> [!NOTE]
> This page is for a teacher, a youth leader, or anyone who wants knowledge that adults wrote retold for readers aged 8 to 18.

Adults write most knowledge for other adults: a field guide, a safety policy, a maths syllabus, the rules of a sport. A school book is that knowledge written again for a younger reader. Knowledge Trust Ladder (KTL) keeps both in one bundle. The adult concept stays as its authors wrote it. A *retelling* says the same thing in shorter words for readers in a band of ages, links back to the adult concept, and carries a trust label of its own.

## What a retelling is

A retelling is a new concept, never an edit of the adult one. It leaves detail out, explains each term, and may reorder what it keeps. It never says what the adult concept does not say.

| Band | The reader | A sentence is split over | A paragraph is split over |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `8-12` | aged 8 to 12 | 15 words | 50 words |
| `13-18` | aged 13 to 18 | 20 words | 75 words |

Ages are the scale, because readers come from many school systems. A school year or a grade is only how a person may say it, and the skill's [young readers page](../skills/ktl-prose/references/young-readers.md#the-bands) maps each to a band. The same page gives a pass for dyslexic readers, which keeps every fact and changes the page in place.

A retelling names the adult concept as its source. So when the adult concept changes, the retelling is on the librarian's work list, and the two cannot drift apart unseen.

## Who does what

| Role | For a retelling |
| --- | --- |
| [**Librarian**](cast/librarian.md) | Writes the retelling from the adult concept alone, as a new concept under `young-readers/`. It writes one when a person asks. On a schedule it writes one only for a band the curation policy names, and only from a concept a person has confirmed |
| [`ktl-prose`](cast/prose.md) | Holds the retelling to its band's limits, and shows by script that it adds nothing the adult concept does not hold |
| [**Curator**](cast/curator.md) | An adult confirms the retelling against the adult concept, and asks whether that concept supports every sentence |
| [**Docent**](cast/docent.md) | Answers in the band the asker names, when the asker says the answer is for a younger reader |
| [**Registrar**](cast/registrar.md) | Checks a retelling as it checks any concept |

## How to ask

- **For a page outside the bundle**: "Write a version of this page for readers aged 13 to 18." `ktl-prose` writes a new file beside the page, such as `README.en-simple.md`, and the page itself stays as it is.
- **For a concept in the bundle**: "Retell the concept on the water cycle for readers aged 8 to 12." The librarian writes it through the pen.
- **For every confirmed concept, on a schedule**: set `Younger readers: 8-12` in the curation policy, with `ktl-curator`.
- **For one answer**: tell the docent who the answer is for, as in "Explain this for my class of ten-year-olds."

Name the band in the request. When a request names no age, grade or school year, the skill asks which band is meant.

## What KTL never does

- **It never guesses an age.** The skills never infer a reader's age from how they write, and never ask a reader's own age. The person who asks names the band.
- **It never records a reader.** No file holds a reader's name, age or words. A host that reads a retelling with young readers records only a count in a band, such as "read with two readers in the band for 8 to 12".
- **It never asks for consent on a host's behalf.** Consent from the readers, and from their parents or carers, stays with the host.

## What exists today

This repository has tried retellings on its own pages only. [The architecture page for readers aged 13 to 18](architecture.en-simple.md) is one, and the bundle holds one retold concept, the pen. [Captured runs](examples/prose.md) show both being written and checked. We know of no school or youth group that has built a bundle this way.

## The other end of the range

A retelling for a ten-year-old and a policy for a bank's auditor sit on the same ladder. Each says where it came from, what checked it, and who vouched for it. [Three lines of defence](three-lines.md) is this page's counterpart for regulated industries.
